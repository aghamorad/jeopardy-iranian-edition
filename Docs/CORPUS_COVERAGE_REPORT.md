### Historiographical Assessment by Period

#### 1. Safavid (1501–1736) [7 Sources — STRONG BASELINE]
- **Key Texts:** Savory (*Iran Under the Safavids*), Matthee (*Persia in Crisis*), Amanat (*Iran: A Modern History*), Katouzian (*The Persians*), Arjomand (*The Turban for the Crown*).
- **Strengths:** Excellent coverage of state foundation (Ismail I, Shi'i conversion, Qizilbash civil wars), the peak under Shah Abbas I, Isfahan urbanism, and Matthee's unrivaled economic-monetary analysis of late Safavid decline and the 1722 Afghan siege.

#### 2. Afsharid (1736–1747) [3 Sources — SPECIALIZED & FOCUSED]
- **Key Texts:** Axworthy (*The Sword of Persia*), Amanat, Katouzian.
- **Strengths:** Michael Axworthy’s definitive military biography provides complete tactical, political, and institutional depth for Nader Shah, the Battle of Karnal, the sacking of Delhi, and Nader's religious Ja'fari madhhab diplomacy.
- **Weakness:** Limited social and everyday life documentation outside of Nader's military apparatus.

#### 3. Zand (1747–1779) [3 Sources — SPECIALIZED & FOCUSED]
- **Key Texts:** Perry (*Karim Khan Zand*), Amanat, Katouzian.
- **Strengths:** John R. Perry's monograph is the definitive Western treatment of Shiraz as capital, Karim Khan’s *Vakil al-Ro'aya* title, tribal confederacies, and Persian Gulf commerce.

#### 4. Qajar Era (1796–1925) [14 Sources — VERY STRONG]
- **Key Texts:** Amanat (*Pivot of the Universe*), Kashani-Sabet (*Frontier Fictions*), Najmabadi (*Women with Mustaches*), Amin (*Making of Modern Iranian Woman*), Browne (*The Persian Revolution*).
- **Strengths:** Deep coverage of Nasir al-Din Shah’s court, Amir Kabir’s reforms, border treaties (Golestan, Turkmenchay), gender aesthetics, telegraph integration, concession protests (Reuter, Tobacco).

#### 5. Constitutional Revolution (1905–1911) [13 Sources — EXCEPTIONAL DEPTH]
- **Key Texts:** Browne (*The Persian Revolution*), Afary (*The Iranian Constitutional Revolution*), Bayat (*Iran's First Revolution*), Abrahamian (*Iran Between Two Revolutions*).
- **Strengths:** World-class historiographical debate represented: contemporary British liberal eyewitness (Browne), subaltern social/feminist council analysis (Afary), and intellectual/clerical confrontation (Bayat on Sheikh Fazlollah Nuri vs Akhund Khorasani).

#### 6. Pahlavi Era: Reza Shah (1921–1941) [18 Sources — VERY STRONG]
- **Key Texts:** Cronin (*The Making of Modern Iran*, *The Army and the Creation of the Pahlavi State*, *Soldiers, Shahs and Subalterns*), Grigor (*Building Iran*), Naficy (Vol. 1).
- **Strengths:** Complete institutional analysis of army formation, Kashf-e Hejab (forced unveiling), architectural revivalism, tribal sedentarization, and subaltern mutinies.

#### 7. 1941–1953 & The 1953 Coup [20 Sources — PEAK DEPTH & RIVAL STANCES]
- **Key Texts:** Abrahamian (*The Coup*), Gasiorowski & Byrne (*Mohammad Mosaddeq and the 1953 Coup*), Katouzian (*Musaddiq and the Struggle for Power*), Mohammad Reza Pahlavi (*Mission for My Country*), Milani (*The Shah*), Behrooz (*Rebels with a Cause*).
- **Strengths:** Unprecedented multi-perspective grounding. Compares CIA/MI6 declassified records (Gasiorowski/Byrne) against anti-imperialist oil hegemony arguments (Abrahamian) and the Shah's personal self-justification.

#### 8. Late Pahlavi & 1979 Revolution [22 Sources — SCHOLARLY CLIMAX]
- **Key Texts:** Khomeini (*Islam and Revolution*), Alam (*The Shah and I*), Mohammad Reza Pahlavi (*Answer to History*), Milani (*The Shah*), Afkhami (*The Life and Times of the Shah*), Bakhash (*The Reign of the Ayatollahs*), Arjomand (*The Turban for the Crown*), Parsa (*Social Origins*), Katouzian (*Political Economy*).
- **Strengths:** Multiple competing paradigms: sociological structuralism (Parsa), petro-state pseudo-modernism (Katouzian), elite royalist court politics (Alam), psycho-biography (Milani), and revolutionary Islamic theology (Khomeini).

#### 9. Islamic Republic (1980s–Present) [17 Sources — HIGHLY COMPETENT]
- **Key Texts:** Axworthy (*Revolutionary Iran*), Harris (*A Social Revolution*), Keshavarzian (*Bazaar and State*), Naficy (Vols. 3 & 4), Siamdoust (*Soundtrack of the Revolution*), Bajoghli (*Iran Reframed*).
- **Strengths:** Exceptional cultural coverage (cinema, music, state propaganda) alongside political economy and welfare state expansion.
- **Weakness:** Contemporary 2010s–2020s diplomacy and street movements (e.g. 2019 Aban, 2022 Mahsa Amini protests) are covered in overview by Axworthy and Bajoghli but lack dedicated monographs.

## 6. Page-Number Provenance Protocol

A game clue is only as strong as its historical citation. To guarantee verifiable provenance:
1. **Running Header / Footer Matching:** Our extraction pipeline strips running headers and footers while capturing printed page numbers.
2. **Page Offset Mapping:** A mapping table in `corpus.db` records the mathematical offset between PDF physical page index (e.g. `p. 45`) and internal printed page number (e.g. `p. 29` after roman numeral frontmatter).
3. **EPUB Page Markers:** For `Abrahamian, The Coup (2013)`, the pipeline parses `id="page_..."` tags directly from chapter HTML.
4. **Clue Attribution Standard:** Every clue in `QuestionBank/` references:
   - `source_id`
   - `book_title`
   - `chapter_title`
   - `page_number`
   - `supporting_passage` (verbatim excerpt of 20–50 words)
   - `fact_status` (`established_fact`, `scholarly_interpretation`, or `primary_testimony`)
